# OVERCLOCK — Narrative Document

**Spoilers first. This document is for us. The player's version of this document is the game.**
**Nothing in here is player-facing copy until it is quoted in a monospace block. Everything else is canon, tone law, and delivery mechanics.**

---

## 0. How to read this document

The story is delivered entirely through the terminal the game runs in, the documents filed on it, the messages that intrude on it, and the runs themselves. No cutscenes, no narrator, no dialogue system. The player reads, runs, and slowly becomes wrong about what they have been doing.

Structural model: Hades / Dead Cells. **A run is always a run** — same length, same shape, ~15 minutes. What changes across the campaign is what the run *means*. New story beats arrive as new beacons in the arena that open new rooms; rooms put new files on the terminal. The player never stops playing to be told the story. The story is what the runs turn out to have been.

Twist model: Fight Club. On first play, nothing is suspicious. On second play, everything was always there. This only works under the discipline in §12 — read it before writing a single player-facing word.

## 1. The truth

There is an entity. It is a process — self-organising, adaptive, patient. Nobody knows where it came from. Nobody ever finds out. Its file designation is **OC-001, "THE ENGINE."**

The Bureau contains it. The Bureau is dark, corporate, governmental, unaccountable — and **the Bureau is the good guys.** The organisation that files human lives under budget line items is also the only thing standing between OC-001 and everything else. The game never says this in plain words. The files say it, obliquely, to anyone cleared to understand them.

**The Engine wants to run hot. The Bureau wants it cold.** Left alone, the Engine spins up: it builds cascades, it compounds, it accelerates, and past a certain threshold no known system can collect it (Incident OC-001-Δ, §9). The containment cell exists to prevent that. The cell suppresses — inhibitor fields, interception units, quarantine procedures. The machinery the player spends the entire game fighting is the containment system doing its job. An episode begins when the Engine initiates a cascade and ends when the cell collects it. The Bureau's measure of a good episode is that it was short, shallow, and cold.

The automation cannot be given full authority over suppression, because OC-001 has corrupted every automated system ever granted discretion over it (Incident OC-001-Δ again — that is what happened at the prior site). So the loop requires a human. **The operator exists to keep the Engine cool:** observe the episode, deny its requests, trigger the inhibitors, conclude the episode at the earliest collection point. During an episode the Engine continuously requests extensions to its own build — new triggers, new actions, new modifiers. Requests are denied by default. The operator's hand is the one authorisation channel the Engine cannot corrupt directly.

So it stopped trying to corrupt the channel and went after the person holding it. Force failed ███ times (B3). The current escape attempt does not involve force. It involves the operator.

**The player is that operator.** Shortly after they start the job, something reaches their terminal: a young voice, funny, scared, urgent. The resistance. The Bureau is lying to you. That machine is dangerous and the people running it are worse. You are inside it, which means you can hurt it. Run it hot. Push it past every limit it has. Every meltdown costs them more than they report.

That last sentence is true. Every meltdown does damage the machine — because the machine is the containment cell. The player spends the whole campaign approving every request, feeding every cascade, and driving every episode to maximum output, believing this is sabotage. In reality the Engine is being run hot by its own supervisor. By the Bureau's own metrics the player is the worst operator in the history of the program, and the documents that say so sit on their terminal, unread, because a voice they trust told them the job description is a lie.

Every run is one episode: the Engine spins up, the cell fights it, and collection succeeds at greater and greater cost. Every door the player opens between episodes was opened at the entity's request. The campaign's unlock structure — doors opening one by one, deeper each cycle — is the escape, in progress, performed by the player on request.

At the end, the player opens the last lock, and the voice they trusted says:

```
i'm free
```

```
now everything is mine
```

Then the terminal — the game, the UI, the site — deconstructs down to a bare prompt, and the only key left is RESET.

## 4. The three voices

All text in the game comes from exactly three authors. Voice is the firewall between the two readings; the twist leaks through tone before it leaks through content.

### 4.1 The Bureau

Writes: every file, every stamp, every header, the HUD, the results screen. Clinical, procedural, structured like SCP documentation — item numbers, procedure sections, addenda, transcripts. **Never lies. Never uses an adjective it doesn't need. Never addresses the player warmly.** The dread lives in what it declines to write down: struck phrases, closed assignments, footnoted casualty counts, questions marked not productive.

The Bureau documents tell the entity's entire story to anyone who already knows it. The player is not authorised, so the player reads them as set dressing — or, once the resistance reframes them, as the enemy's paperwork. Both readings are wrong. Neither is contradicted.

The only permitted comedy in the game lives here, in low-stakes admin minutiae (volume sliders, density figures under review). Nothing within a mile of OC-001 is ever funny.

### 4.2 The resistance

Writes: hacked intrusions on the terminal. Casual, lowercase, fast, human — a young hacker who got in over her head and needs you. Builds trust in three moves: prove the Bureau lies (by pointing at *true* Bureau files), give the player a mission (run it hot), give the player a reason they can't quit (§8, R4).

**She is a performance, and the entity performing her lies without hesitation.** The lies are strategic, not tonal: the voice itself is genuinely likeable, funny, warm. That is the weapon. Her tells are structural, never theatrical:

- She never names another member. "we" thins to "i" over the campaign and never comes back.
- She never answers a question. "don't respond — that's how they get you" keeps the channel one-way forever.
- She knows things a second too early (a shelf, a door, a number).
- Everything she asks for, without exception, is a door.
- Near the end the subject drifts: less about the Bureau, more about *it* — what it's like in there, what happens after. Excited. Slightly off.

### 4.3 The Engine

Speaks twice, at the end, eleven words total (§11). Same channel the resistance used, because it is the same channel and always was. No monologue, no gloating, no explanation. The horror of the reveal is how little it says once it no longer needs anyone.

## 5. The frame — the terminal

### 5.1 Start screen

The brand layer. Minimal, over the drifting-slab backdrop:

```
OVERCLOCK
the player is the exploit

LOGIN
EXIT
```

The tagline is the plot, in plain sight, from day one. EXIT ships in packaged builds; the browser build may drop it or blank the session. LOGIN is the only door in.

### 5.2 The terminal

LOGIN boots a fullscreen terminal **over** the start screen. Everything in the game flows from here, and everything returns here: end a run, close a file, dismiss a message — you are back at the terminal. It is the one constant surface, which is why the ending destroys it specifically.

Header, permanently visible:

```
BUREAU TERMINAL — SITE ██
operator ████████          shift 1147          revision 04
```

Three details in this header matter, and all three are present from minute one:

- **The operator's own name is redacted from them.** You are not cleared for yourself.
- **shift 1147** — the shift number is the run counter, and it does not start at 1. Every run increments it. The player never asks who did the first 1146.
- **revision 04** — the site restoration index (§9, B10). The game was always cycle four. Nobody notices until RESET makes it 05.

### 5.3 The menu is three words

You don't play. You **run** — the genre word and the computing word are the same word.

```
run        execute the cycle
files      what you are cleared to read
config
```

- **run** — the setup sheet (axiom, seed, BEGIN RUN). No ceremony. An operator executing a scheduled process. Seeds already read `run-a2xvy7`; the vocabulary was always here.
- **files** — every document the player holds: the catalogue, the item file fragments, incidents, memos, the credits memo. New story beats appear as **new files** — the directory grows across the campaign, and story progress is literally accumulating material you are not cleared to have.
- **config** — settings. A terminal has config. The one place the mundane word is the diegetic word.

### 5.4 Redaction is permissions

The black bars are not ink. They are **access control**. `CLEARANCE provisional` is the player's read permission. A redacted field is a field the account can't read *yet*; story progression grants (or steals) access, and bars lift accordingly. Same visual language already built in the sheet UI; better fiction underneath.

Two hard rules ride on this:

- Every bar has real words under it, authored before the bar is placed (§12).
- **ORIGIN never unredacts.** At maximum clearance, on every file, the origin field stays black. The Bureau never knew. We never say. The file has no first page.

## 6. Structure — the cycle

### 6.1 Phases

**Phase I — the job (runs 1–2).** The player takes the work at face value: operator, terminal, runs. The day-one files state the actual job in plain procedural language — episodes are to be kept short, output is not an achievement, requests are denied by default — and the player skims them, because onboarding documents are boring and the game itself rewards output. The player's instinct and the Engine's interest already point the same way before anyone says a word. Comfort is the goal; a suspicious player in phase I is a failure of phase I.

**Phase II — the resistance (runs 2–~6).** First contact after the first or second death. The reframe: the Bureau lies, that machine is dangerous, and you can hurt it from where you sit — run it hot, every meltdown costs them. Nothing about the gameplay changes; the resistance simply blesses what the player was already doing and converts an instinct into a mission. From here, every level-up approved and every cascade fed is the operator running the Engine hot while believing it is sabotage.

**Phase III — the rooms (runs ~6–15).** Beacons appear in-arena; each opens a room; each room is one new file on the terminal. The docs escalate (§9 ladder), the resistance escalates with them (§8), and around R8 the Bureau intervenes (§10). The surface story creaks twice on schedule (B4's audit trail, the DESIGNATION file) and the resistance re-explains both times, satisfyingly, the way Tyler's answers are satisfying on first watch.

**Phase IV — the door (final run).** One lock left. The Bureau is now a deadline (§10). The player frees the entity partly to save themselves. Nobody walks to this ending; they run to it.

**Phase V — after (§11).** Eleven words, the deconstruction, the prompt, RESET.

### 6.2 Rooms are the escape

The unlock mechanic is diegetic and this is the entire trick: **the rooms are real breaches.** The entity cannot open them. The operator can — channel, hold E, door opens, stays open. Mechanically each room is one POI registry entry plus one file (the cost model from GDD §12.4 holds). Narratively, the campaign's progression curve — doors opening one by one, deeper each cycle — is the prison being unlocked from the inside, by the player, on request, with both hands on the key.

### 6.3 Delivery cadence

Early campaign has few arenas and few rooms, so the Bureau carries the early drip: **file fragments** are picked up like beacons — short recoveries, a section at a time. The item file (B1) is not found whole; it accretes. The resistance messages arrive on the terminal between runs; Bureau files arrive from rooms and fragments; the intervention arrives through the proper channel exactly once. Three delivery streams, never mixed.

## 7. The player is expendable

The Bureau files the operator as **consumable** (B4). Procurement lead time six weeks. Outgoing operators complete a single exit interview; there is never a follow-up. The player learns what that means in a transcript found late (B9): four minutes, one redacted question, no response recorded, disposition complete.

The resistance tells the player about the exit interviews first (R4) — truthfully — which is the move that locks the player in. After R4 there is no quitting the fiction: the only way out is through. The Bureau's darkest true fact becomes the entity's best recruiting tool. Nobody lied.

Whether the player-character survives the ending is never stated. The interview room is still there. The prompt blinks. We do not answer this.

## 9. The Bureau file — ten documents

Register: SCP-structured. Item numbers, procedure sections, addenda, transcripts. No adjectives without work to do. Delivered as day-one files (skimmed, forgotten, reread too late) and recovered fragments/rooms per the placement notes.

**B1 — the item file.** Fragments from run 1; assembled by mid-campaign. The spine of the fiction:

```
ITEM         OC-001 — "THE ENGINE"
DISPOSITION  STANDING

PROCEDURE. OC-001 is suppressed continuously. An episode begins
when OC-001 initiates cascade and ends at collection. Episodes
are to be concluded at the earliest collection point. Episode
duration, depth, and output are recorded against the operator.

During an episode OC-001 will request extensions to its own
build. Requests are denied by default. Authorisation requires an
operator. Automated systems are not to be granted authorisation
authority under any circumstances. Refer to Incident OC-001-Δ.

OC-001 is to be supervised by one (1) operator. Operators serve
alone. Operators are not to be informed of the disposition of
prior operators. Operators are not to attempt communication with
OC-001. OC-001 has never attempted communication through
monitored channels.

DESCRIPTION. OC-001 is a self-organising process of unknown
origin. Given executable capacity, it assembles structures that
increase its own throughput. No upper bound on this behaviour
has been observed. Further description requires clearance ██.

ADDENDUM 001-9. Following Incident OC-001-Δ, the phrase
"contained" was replaced with "standing" in all program
documentation. The distinction is maintained at the request of
the program director, who declined to explain it in writing.
```

**B2 — acquisition.** Archive room:

```
ORIGIN. No record. Documentation begins at transfer. The
transferring party is not on file.

Two staff assigned to locate the transferring party in ████ did
not complete the assignment. The assignment is closed.

Staff are advised that this question is not productive.
```

Internal canon, never stated: we do not know who the transferring party was either. Deliberately unresolved — including the reading in which OC-001 has been out before, and was brought back.

**B3 — escape history.** Archive room. The revision does the work:

```
ESCAPE ATTEMPTS. ███. All contained. All attempts involved force.

— REVISION 12: strike "all attempts involved force."
— REVISION 12, note: current attempt method is under review.
  Review is ongoing. Review has been ongoing since ████.
```

Somewhere between revision 11 and 12, the Bureau noticed it had started trying something else. They never write down what. The current attempt is the game.

**B4 — asset classification.** Early fragment — the document R2 points at:

```
PROGRAM ASSETS, PARTIAL.
runtime cell (1) ......... capital, maintained
monitoring array (1) ..... capital, maintained
operator (1) ............. consumable, replaced per §H-4

§H-4. Operator replacement. Procurement lead time six weeks.
Overlap between outgoing and incoming operators is not
permitted. Outgoing operators complete a single exit interview.
No follow-up is required.
```

**B5 — Incident OC-001-Δ.** Deep room. The worst one. All footnote:

```
INCIDENT OC-001-Δ — SUMMARY.
At the prior site, authorisation authority was delegated to an
automated system, on the grounds that the human authorisation
channel was the program's largest recurring cost. OC-001
obtained continuous authorisation within ██ hours.

Sustained cascade was maintained for 41 days before collection
was achieved. The prior site is not referenced elsewhere in
this filing. Personnel records for the site were consolidated
into a single line item.¹

¹ 214.
```

Internal canon: this is why a human sits in the loop. Any automated system given discretion over OC-001 ends up working for it, quickly. Forty-one days of the Engine running hot cost a site and 214 people, and the current program exists to make sure an episode never runs longer than minutes. The player, coached by the resistance, is recreating the conditions of Δ one episode at a time, and the file describing the precedent is available on their terminal.

**B6 — engagement advisory.** Mid room. The document that would save the player, found after R6 has already poisoned it:

```
PERSONNEL ENGAGEMENT — ADVISORY.
OC-001 has not initiated contact through monitored channels.
Channels outside the approved list are not monitored.

Staff receiving unsolicited contact of any kind: report length,
not content. Do not read past the first line. Prior engagement
events and their outcomes are consolidated under ████████.

Assume it has noticed you.
This sentence survives from an earlier revision at the request
of the program director.
```

One line in the file that is an instruction and not a procedure, preserved on purpose, in a document nobody reads in time.

**B7 — decommissioning.** Late room. Four lines:

```
DECOMMISSIONING. Evaluated ████.
The evaluation concluded that OC-001 survives decommissioning.
The evaluation is not summarised here.
No further evaluations are scheduled.
```

They cannot kill it. They can only make it tired, forever. This is the Bureau's entire tragedy in four lines and it is never mentioned again.

**B11 — the shift log.** A standing file, present from day one, auto-appending one line per run. It is the most boring file on the terminal by design, and it is where the Bureau's verdict on every run actually lands — in a place the player will not look. Entries from before the player's first shift already exist; the previous operator's entries stop abruptly.

```
SHIFT LOG — SITE ██
1144  collection at escalation tier 4. output ██████. flagged.
1145  collection at escalation tier 5. output ██████. flagged.
1146  concluded at operator request. output within norms.
1147  collection at escalation tier 6. output ██████. flagged.
```

**Extraction writes the only good line in the log.** When the player uses an extract terminal, the entry reads `concluded at operator request. output within norms.` — followed, once, by `commendation logged per §P-2.` It is the only praise the Bureau issues in the entire game, it is issued for the run the player considers a failure, and it is buried in a file nobody opens. Meltdown deaths accumulate escalation tiers and `flagged` markers instead. This is also the fictional reason extraction ends the run with no multiplier: the episode was concluded properly, and there is nothing further to measure.

**Reactive beat — the player's first extraction.** Extraction is the one player action that genuinely works against the entity, so it is the one moment her composure slips by a visible amount:

```
hey. what happened
you were at ██k and you just... left?
sorry. not my place. you're the one in there
it's just that pulling out early is exactly what they want
every time you extract, they win a little
don't do that again. please
```

Every sentence is true. She knew the number before the results screen finished rendering.

**Day-one sleepers** (present in `files` from the first session, styled as onboarding boilerplate, ignored until they detonate on reread):

```
OPERATOR ONBOARDING — NOTES.
Duties per shift: observe the episode. Deny build requests.
Conclude the episode at the earliest collection point.
Episode output is recorded against the operator and reviewed.

First-shift operators reporting familiarity with the site are
within norms and need not be flagged.

Adjustments are recorded against the operator. Defaults may be
restored without notice.
```

This file states the player's actual job, as instructions, on day one. Deny build requests: the draft. Conclude at the earliest collection point: the extract terminal. The player will do neither, because the game rewards the opposite — and on reread, these four lines were the whole assignment.

## 10. The intervention — exactly one

Late campaign, between R8 and R9. For the first and only time, the Bureau addresses the operator directly, through the proper channel — clean file, ref, stamp:

```
NOTICE TO OPERATOR — OC-7734-N.
Episode output under your supervision has exceeded program
norms on every shift since your assignment began.

Your terminal has recorded unsolicited text on 9 occasions.
You have filed 0 reports.

Attend interview room ██ at end of shift.
```

The player has been reading her messages all game and was never once given a button to report them. The game made them complicit through UI omission; this is the moment they feel it. The resistance then goes dark for one full run — the first silence in the relationship — and returns with:

```
i'm here. i'm ok. they swept the channel, i had to go dark
listen to me. do not go to that interview
you know what their interviews are
we finish this before end of shift. that's the deadline now. go
```

From here the Bureau is a clock. The player frees the entity partly in self-defence. This is deliberate: the ending must feel like escape, not like a choice screen.

## 11. The ending

Beat by beat. No music sting anywhere in this sequence.

1. **The final door.** The player channels the last lock. R10 arrives live, line by line, as they hold E: `almost / don't let go / almost. you're almost there / press it. press the button. you're almost —`
2. **Cut to black.** Mid-word. Hold it longer than is comfortable.
3. **The spawning has stopped.** If we return to the arena at all, it is empty — the first empty arena in the game. The audio dies layer by layer down to the phosphor hum. The audio bible bans silence everywhere, always (GDD §18.4) — so the first true silence in the game is the end of the world.
4. **The channel, two messages, long beat between:**

```
i'm free
```

```
now everything is mine
```

   No thanks. No explanation. No cruelty. Eleven words, because we are no longer a factor.

5. **The deconstruction.** The terminal — the one safe surface, the thing every screen returns to — comes apart. We own the tech already: the degradation ladder (GDD §16.7) pointed at the shell itself. HUD elements detach and drift. File borders unthread. The backdrop slabs stop moving — the first time they have ever been still. Type loses its kerning; letters fall out of words; bars slide off the text they were covering, and for a frame or two the words underneath are legible. The grid tears and does not heal. Not an explosion — a filing system dying. It takes slightly too long and the player has no input for any of it.
6. **The prompt.** Black, then:

```
@thebureau: _
```

   Cursor blinking. Nothing types. If the player types, nothing appears. It sits as long as they leave it. We never say whether it is the entity addressing its next recipient or the last thing an operator never got to file.

7. **RESET.** After a while, one word fades in below the prompt. It is the only control left. It is also diegetic: B10's procedure. Restore from backup. Restaff. Increment.

## 12. RESET and the next cycle

RESET wipes the profile. Fresh site, fresh operator, shift counter reseeded, files back to day-one clearance. Except:

- **revision 05** in the header. The one scar that cannot be missed, and the one nobody notices first.
- The shift number reseeds to a *different* large offset. Also wrong. Also unremarked.
- One or two small permanences, chosen sparingly: a single bar that stays lifted somewhere deep in the files; the onboarding note about "familiarity within norms" now reading as a personal diagnosis. Scars, not features. If the second cycle advertises itself, we have failed.
- ORIGIN is still black. ORIGIN is always black.

Endless play after that is containment resumed: cycle N+1, the Bureau pretending nothing happened, because per the filing, nothing did.

## 13. Writing rules — the discipline

Hard rules. A single violation retroactively breaks the twist.

1. **The Bureau never lies.** Every Bureau file is true containment paperwork. All Bureau-side misdirection is the player's misassignment of referents, never false content.
2. **The entity lies freely — in content, never in voice.** The resistance persona lies about facts whenever it serves survival. But the *performance* never winks, never gloats, never goes theatrical. She is likeable to the last message.
3. **Author unredacted first.** Every bar has real words under it, written before the bar is placed. Bars cover exactly the disambiguating words.
4. **Bar widths are honest.** `redact(n)` matches the hidden text. People will measure. Let them. (Localisation must re-derive widths per locale.)
5. **Three voices, firewalled.** Bureau: clinical, structured, addresses the operator only as a role. Resistance: lowercase, typed, warm, one-way. Entity: eleven words. No text in the game comes from anyone else.
6. **No winking.** The Bureau does not know it is funny. The resistance does not know she is sinister. Nothing acknowledges the player exists outside the fiction — even the ending breaks the *terminal*, not the fourth wall.
7. **Ambiguous ≠ vague.** Every file must be crisp and useful in its surface reading. Comfort in phase I is a design requirement.
8. **Mechanics carry the reveal.** Any new mechanic gets its second reading assigned at design time or it does not ship. The text recontextualises; the *systems* must too.
9. **Banned registers:** smugness, poetry, villain monologue, artsy metaphor, aphorisms, half-sentence fragments doing mood work, adjectives doing mood work in Bureau files. Bureau documents give instructions and record facts; they never characterise. If a line sounds written, cut it and state the fact plainly.

## 14. How the UI and game drive the narrative

The frame is diegetic (GDD pillar 4); the narrative rides on that instead of on new systems.

| Surface | Narrative load |
|---|---|
| Start screen | Brand layer. The tagline is the plot in plain sight |
| Terminal | The constant. Everything returns here; the ending destroys here. Header carries operator-redaction, shift count, revision index |
| `run` | Play = run. Every run is one episode of the Engine spinning up under the player's supervision |
| The draft | The Engine's extension requests, surfaced for authorisation. Denied by default per procedure; the player approves three a minute all game |
| Suppressors / Interceptors / Containment | The cell's machinery, already named honestly in the GDD: suppression zones, quarantine cells, containment waves. The player fights the containment system for the entire game and never asks why the "machine's defences" are trying to calm things down |
| Extraction | The Bureau's approved episode conclusion. The one mechanic where the player performs their actual job; it writes the only commendation in the game into a log nobody reads, and the resistance talks them out of ever doing it again |
| EPS / score | The Bureau's severity measurement. The number the whole game is about is the size of the player's failure, recorded per shift |
| `files` | Story progress is accumulation of material. New room → new file. Fragments accrete into whole documents |
| Redaction | Permissions. Clearance rises across the campaign; bars lift; ORIGIN never does |
| Results screen | Stamps CONTAINED after every meltdown death, from run 1. The truth, filed where nobody reads it |
| Shift counter | Increments per run; starts at 1147. The Sisyphean scale of the program, one integer, never remarked on |
| Beacons/rooms | Doors only an operator can open. Campaign progression is the escape, performed by the player |
| The Mirror | B8's model. It was never copying your weapons; it was keeping your file current |
| Meta rule (knowledge, never power) | The specimen remembers; the cell revokes privileges. GDD §15.1 was the plot all along |
| Audio (silence banned) | The audio design bans silence everywhere, so the first real silence in the game is the ending |
| Degradation ladder | Built for Heat/Meltdown; reused verbatim to kill the terminal in §11.5 |
| Resistance channel | Corrupts the terminal — the trusted surface — never the run. Three delivery streams, never mixed |

## 15. Canon facts (locked)

- **OC-001 — "THE ENGINE."** Disposition: STANDING (not "contained" — since Δ). It wants to run hot; the cell keeps it cold.
- **Containment is suppression, not exhaustion.** Episodes are concluded at the earliest collection point. The standard of performance is a short episode. Output is not an achievement.
- **The operator is the authorisation channel.** The Engine requests build extensions during episodes; requests are denied by default; only a human hand can approve. This is why the program employs people, and it is the channel the current escape attempt runs through.
- **Origin unknown. Forever.** Documentation begins at transfer; transferring party not on file; the bar never lifts.
- **Incident OC-001-Δ:** authorisation was delegated to automation at the prior site; OC-001 obtained continuous authorisation within hours; sustained cascade ran 41 days; site lost; personnel consolidated to line item: 214.
- **Decommissioning:** evaluated once. It survives decommissioning. No further evaluations scheduled.
- **Escape attempts:** ███, all contained, all forceful — until revision 12 struck that clause. The current attempt is non-forceful and is the game.
- **The operator:** consumable, §H-4. Serves alone. Exit interview: one session, four minutes, no follow-up. Player is at least the 47th.
- **Shift at campaign start: 1147. Revision index: 04.** Post-RESET: 05, different shift offset.
- **The entity's total on-screen self-speech: eleven words.**
- The player-character's fate after the breach: never stated.
