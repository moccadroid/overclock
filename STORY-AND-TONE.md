# OVERCLOCK — Story and Tone

**This file is the reference for what the story is and how the two voices sound.**
NARRATIVE.md holds the canon and the twist discipline. This holds the *arc as it
will actually be built*, the escalation ladder, sample artifacts at publication
standard, and the writing constraints that produced them.

Where this contradicts NARRATIVE.md, this wins, and NARRATIVE.md gets amended:
the disposition word, the placement of the predecessor's file, and the room
number all moved here.

Written 2026-08-07 after a review pass that rejected the previous text wholesale.
§6 explains what was wrong with it. Read §6 before writing a single line of
either voice.

---

## 1. The story

An entity exists. Nobody knows where it came from and nobody ever finds out.
Filed as OC-001. It is a process that assembles structures to increase its own
throughput, and it has no observed upper bound. Left running, it compounds until
nothing can collect it.

The Bureau contains it. The Bureau is unaccountable, cold, and correct.
Containment is not a cage, it is a continuous act: the thing spins up, the cell
suppresses it, and an operator concludes the episode. Every twenty-two minutes
of a hot episode is a debt somebody pays later. Forty-one days of it cost a site
and two hundred and fourteen people, once, at a place that is no longer
referenced by name.

The reason a human sits in the loop is that OC-001 has corrupted every automated
system ever given discretion over it. So the loop requires a hand. The
operator's hand is the one authorisation channel the entity cannot reach
directly, and during an episode it asks constantly — new triggers, new actions,
new modifiers. Procedure says deny. The player approves three a minute.

Force failed nine times. The current attempt is not force. It is a person: warm,
competent, funny, extremely good at what she does, and she has been in their
network long enough to read their filing. She tells the player the Bureau is
lying, and points at documents that are true. She tells them to run the machine
hot because every meltdown costs the Bureau something. That is also true. What
she does not say is that the machine is the containment cell and the cost is
paid in the only currency that matters.

### 1.1 The spine

**The Bureau's documents escalate across the campaign, and the escalation is not
malice. It is recognition.** They have seen this exact signature before — an
operator whose output climbs, whose terminal records unsolicited text, who stops
concluding episodes early. There is an instruction for it. The instruction ends
in a room. So the files move from flat description of the job, to monitoring, to
concern, to a scheduled appointment, and the player reads that curve as a
company closing in on them, because that is exactly what it looks like from
inside.

Both readings are load-bearing to the last minute:

- **Surface.** An organisation that filed you under equipment has been measuring
  you, has decided you are a problem, and wants you in a room nobody comes back
  from. Your only friend is telling you to finish tonight because tomorrow you
  are dead.
- **Actual.** Containment officers watching a compromise replay, following a
  procedure written after the last one, moving at the speed institutions move.
  They intend to kill the operator. They are right to. They lose because process
  is slower than a person who has decided to run.

The player is never given a choice screen. By the last run they are fleeing an
execution and completing the escape, and those are the same action.

### 1.2 Canon changes from NARRATIVE.md

- **Disposition is INDEFINITE**, not "standing". The words *standing*, *held* and
  *partial* are banned everywhere player-facing. File stamps are CONFIDENTIAL.
- **The interview room is 02A.** Redacted as `02█` in every early file. She says
  it in the clear *before* any file the player holds unredacts it. The final
  notice prints it. The bar lifts exactly once, and that is the threat arriving.
- **The predecessor's exit interview and the disposal instruction sit mid-arc**,
  not at the end. The final notice only lands if the player already knows
  precisely what the appointment is.
- **Clause 4.1 of OC-0052 is the hinge of the design**: findings are withheld
  because disclosure accelerates the finding. It reads as a company hiding test
  results. It means naming the compromise helps it along, which is also why the
  room number is redacted until it is not.

## 3. Her

She breached the most secure network in the world. She is not a nervous kid, she
does not say lol, and she is not talking — she is typing into a terminal she does
not have permission to be in, usually with a window that is closing. What makes
her dangerous is that she is *good*, and that she is kind to the player without
needing anything visible in return.

She quotes the Bureau's paperwork back at them fluently. They never quote her;
they log her length.

**First contact.** She does not introduce herself and does not pretend the
player can verify anything.

```
this channel isn't on their approved list. that's the only reason it works
i'm not going to tell you who i am. you couldn't check it if i did
what i can do is tell you where to look
they keep records in the west bays, past the first partition
find the asset schedule. read what it says on the line with your job title
i'll be here tomorrow, same time, whether or not you looked
don't reply. it only goes one direction and an attempt to answer will flag it
```

**After the schedule.** Warmth arrives as attention, which is also the tell.

```
you found it
"consumable, per H-4". same schedule as the inhibitor stock, two lines down from the reserve
i've read a lot of their filing. that isn't sloppiness, somebody chose that word and somebody signed it
different subject. your third episode tonight was very good
i can see the output numbers, not the room, so i don't know how you did it, only that you did
they can see them too. they've started reviewing them, which tells me the number matters to them
so make it bigger
```

**Mid campaign, on the monitoring file.** She takes their language and uses it
as a weapon, without a single flourish.

```
clause 4.1. read it twice
they're not hiding your results to protect you, they say why right there. disclosure accelerates the finding
so there's a finding. on a form, with a number, that you're not allowed to see
i've been trying to pull form 19 for a week. it isn't on the network, which means it's on paper, which means somebody walks it to an office
that's the level of care they're taking with the thing they won't tell you
keep going. i'll keep trying
```

**The deadline.** She names the room before any file the player holds has it
unredacted.

```
they've scheduled you. 02A, end of shift
i've seen the intake forms for that room. there are no outtake forms
i'm not being dramatic and i'm not guessing, it's on their calendar with your assignment number next to it
you have one shift. one lock left
i'll be on the channel the whole time. i'm not going anywhere
```

**On the lock, live, line by line.** No period at the end.

```
almost
don't let go
almost. you're almost there
press it. press the button. you're almost —
```

## 4. The two voices, as constraints

Stated as rules that can be caught being broken, not as advice.

### 4.1 The Bureau

Numbered clauses. Cross-references to instructions and forms by number. Defined
terms in quotation marks, reused exactly. Passive throughout. Sentences of
thirty-five words are normal and nothing is under eight words except a heading.
Hedges: *where practicable, as appropriate, may be required, save where*. No
sentence exists to be quoted. The institution never names its own cruelty and
never editorialises — the atrocity goes in a subordinate clause about
accounting, and the reader finds it. Every document is longer than it needs to
be. Nothing lands.

**Quotas.** At least one sentence over 35 words; two cross-references to other
instructions or forms by number; one defined term in quotes used more than once;
one hedge; no sentence under 8 words except headings.

### 4.2 Her

Lowercase, fast, accurate, because she types for a living. No aphorisms, no
proverbs, no tricolons, no self-commentary, no "and they call it X". She never
explains a joke and rarely makes one. She is operational: she says what she
knows, what she is doing, what she wants. She is specific about their systems
because she has actually been inside them, which is what makes her credible and
is the same reason she knows things she should not. Her warmth is small and
concrete — she noticed a run, she remembers a number, she says she will be
there. She asks nothing and cannot be answered.

**Quotas.** No line quotable out of context. She is competent in every message;
she is never bumbling, never a teenager, never cute. When she is wrong it is
because she is lying, not because she is confused.

### 4.3 How they interlock

She reads their documents and quotes them by clause. They record that she
happened, in bytes. She is the only voice in the game with a second person in
it; every document refers to "the operator". So the player receives one channel
that says *you* and one channel that says *the position*, and picks the one that
seems to know they exist.

## 5. After action — what the reread finds

The onboarding notices on the wall of the first room stated the job correctly on
the first shift: conclude the episode at the earliest opportunity, requests are
denied by default, output is recorded against the operator. The player read them
as tutorial signage, which they were.

Every document the player recovered was true. None were forged, altered, or
planted. What the entity controlled was which four they found first, and the
order. Selection, then the schedule, then monitoring, then the predecessor. Read
in that order the Bureau is a company that files people under equipment and then
disposes of them. Read in any other order it is an organisation that has
learned, expensively, that an operator who becomes attached to OC-001 is a
breach in progress, and that the breach cannot be reversed once the operator
knows it has been diagnosed.

The escalating notices were not a company closing in on an inconvenient
employee. They were clause 2, then clause 3.3, then clause 6, in order, with the
correct forms, at institutional speed. The interview in 02A was the
countermeasure. It has worked forty-six times. It works by removing the hand
before the hand opens the last lock, and it is the only thing that has ever
worked, and everyone involved knows exactly what they are doing when they
schedule it.

She won by two hours.

The last thing worth noting is what she never did. She never opened a door,
never marked a wall, never spawned anything, never touched the cell. She pointed
at true documents and said kind, accurate things about the player's work. Every
request she made was a lock, and every lock needed a hand that was not hers.

## 6. What was wrong before — the slop catalogue

The text this document replaces failed in six identifiable ways. All six are
mechanical and all six recurred in both voices, which is how they were caught:
the Bureau and the hacker had the same tics, so they were the same voice wearing
two hats.

**1. The negation-as-insight.** A sentence whose entire content is a pivot on
"not": *"Output is not a performance measure." "It is not treated." "The model
is not OC-001."* The rhythm implies a revelation and there is no information in
it.

**2. Self-admiring commentary.** A line stepping outside itself to appreciate
the pattern it just described: *"weird rule but it holds", "not in a creepy way
lol", "The reclassification is retroactive."* A clerk writing a revision note
does not land a beat.

**3. "And they call it X."** *"the machine eats people like you on a schedule
and they stamp it 'maintenance'"* — a columnist's outrage, not a person's.

**4. Tricolon and anaphora in dialogue.** *"not staff. not the auditors. no
one"*, *"the machine goes down, the program goes down, the interviews stop"*.
Parallel structure with three escalating beats is oratory. Nobody builds it
while frightened.

**5. Compression as personality.** Dropping words to sound hard-boiled. It reads
as one voice — the author's — in every mouth.

**6. Every paragraph lands.** Nothing trails off, gets bogged down, or ends
because the information ran out. Real documents and real messages do all three.

### 6.1 Banned constructions

1. No sentence whose point is a negation pivot ("X is not Y", "not X but Y").
2. No line that comments on another line's pattern or on its own cleverness.
3. No tricolon or anaphora in dialogue, ever.
4. No paragraph ending on a short reframing clause.
5. No "and they call it X" / "and they stamp it Y".
6. No em-dash pivot in dialogue; at most one per Bureau document.
7. **The institution never names its own cruelty.** No "consent",
   "expendable", "cost of a life" as editorial. Procedure only. When B12 ended
   with "no candidate on it has consented to be on it", that was the author
   pointing at his own villainy so the reader would not miss it, and it killed
   the effect. The absence of the word is the horror.

### 6.2 Three tests

- **Screenshot test** (her): would this look normal in a screenshot of a
  terminal session? If it looks like film dialogue, cut it.
- **Photocopy test** (them): would this survive as page 4 of a 30-page policy
  nobody reads? If it reads like a novel about a policy, cut it.
- **The "nice line" test**: if the writer feels pleased with a sentence, delete
  it. This catches the most.

### 6.3 The instruction that works

Naming an author does not work. **Naming the document type does.** "Write this
as a section of an insurance policy." "Write this as an OSHA citation." "Write
this as an HR handbook page about equipment checkout." For her: "write this as
terminal messages from someone with root on a network they do not work for, who
has about a minute."

### 6.4 The character error

She was written as *cool* — epigrammatic, economical, always landing — while
being described as bubbly and out of her depth. Those are incompatible, and the
rewrite that made her a bumbling teenager was worse. She breached the most
secure network in existence. She is warm **by choice**, which is a far more
effective seduction than vulnerability, and the entity performing "capable
person who has decided to be kind to you" is much more plausible as the thing
that fools forty-seven operators than a scared kid would be.

There is also a craft reason her competence matters. The deliberate tells — the
shelf she should not know, the room number she has before the Bureau prints it —
only hide if the surrounding text is dense with *credible* specifics. Her
knowing the west bays reads as competence on first pass and as impossible
knowledge on the second. A rambling character has no cover to hide a tell in.

## 7. The early-access campaign — six runs, five rooms

Decided 2026-08-07. This is the shipping shape. Free play, dailies, the Mirror,
Recompile, clearance progression and cycle-two content all come later; RESET at
the end of the campaign shows the wishlist screen.

### 7.1 Principles

- **The unit of story is the terminal visit.** Each run has two slots: the
  commitment point (BEGIN RUN — a notice or a message can hold the run) and the
  results screen. Six runs is twelve slots plus in-arena recoveries.
- **Run length is controlled by geography, not by a clock.** Gates stay open
  across runs, so a later run is a longer journey to a further frontier, never a
  repeat of an old exam. Extraction stands at the *current* frontier and is the
  intended ending of every story run. Meltdown exists only so nobody lingers;
  it carries no story.
- **Rooms are 3–4 minutes each.** Only the final run crosses everything:
  12–15 minutes through four gates to the Cell. Total campaign ~50 minutes
  played clean, 70–90 with deaths and reading.
- **The engine grows between runs.** Canon: the build is OC-001's and it
  remembers (the Library rule was always this). Run 1 starts Clock→Bolt; each
  run starts bigger; run 6 starts as the full pre-tuned monster — her gift.
  Fairness is not the point of the final run. Escape velocity is.
- **Hard-gate the rungs, let the colour be missable.** The arc does not advance
  past a rung until its document is held. Every load-bearing document sits
  within seconds of the walking line between spawn and the gate — off-axis
  enough to be a choice, never a search. If something optional is missed, she
  points at it next run (that is her function anyway).
- **Death → CONTINUE → operations order → BEGIN RUN.** No separate retry
  button: the terminal is where beats land, and a retry that skips it skips the
  notice that was waiting. BEGIN RUN is pre-focused so the loop is three keys.

### 7.2 The ladder

| Run | Reaches | Recovers | At BEGIN RUN | At end | ~Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heap | onboarding 1–4 | assignment notice | orientation pass / fail notice | 2–3 min |
| 2 | Sink | asset schedule (OC-0044), selection (OC-0051) | R1 first contact | silence (nag if empty-handed) | ~6 min |
| 3 | Archive | monitoring (OC-0052) | R2 the proof | notice N/1 | ~8 min |
| 4 | Archive, deep bays | exit interview (OC-0046-X) + closure (OC-0031) | R3 clause 4.1 | **dead gate in-run**, then notice N/2 | ~7 min |
| 5 | Store | Δ incident (OC-001-D), engagement advisory (OC-0061), item file completes | R4 the predecessor + the relay | notice N/3 — **02A** | ~12 min |
| 6 | The Cell | the last lock | R5 the gift + the deadline | the ending | 12–15 min |

Run 4 is deliberately the shortest and the worst: the player reads what happened
to operator 46, walks to the next door, and finds it **powered down** — not
sealed, dead. The run has nowhere to go and ends early; the shortness is the
beat. N/2 lands on the way out. It is the first time the Bureau acts on the
player physically instead of on paper.

**The relay (run 5).** She got into the facilities system and unlocked a service
point on the player's side of the wall. The player holds it like a gate; power
returns; the Store partition works again. This is her single physical
intervention in the whole game, permitted because it is *a door* — the one
category of thing she is allowed to want — and she still cannot hold it herself.

**The extraction trap.** The Bureau commends every extraction in the shift log,
because concluding early is the correct action. The player spends four runs
being quietly praised for the thing that is, per procedure, their entire job —
and reads the praise as the company being cheap about their real achievements.

**The Cell has no extraction terminal.** Runs 2, 3 and 5 teach that extraction
stands at the frontier. Run 6's frontier has a lock instead. Nobody says
anything about it.

### 7.3 What is cut from early access

Recompile (code stays, no story on it). The Mirror. Axiom choice (campaign is
Ignition-only; the growing starter is the progression). Clearance progression
(bars are static; exactly one bar lifts in the whole game: `02█` → `02A`).
Free play, dailies, custom seeds, endless. RESET → wishlist.

## 8. The visual descent

Decided 2026-08-07, superseding the earlier field-based plan.

### 8.1 Forbidden

**The existing screen-space "biome field" programs — frost, ember, static — are
forbidden. They read as a children's cartoon. Remove them on sight.** No
authored screen-space set-dressing of that kind, ever. Screen space is allowed
exactly one honest category: weather — dust, ash, snow, rain — sparse, drifting,
subtle, across the whole frame.

### 8.2 The rule

**Effects live ON the ruins.** The ruins are the game's visual identity; they
are where colour, glow and wrongness enter. The escalation is the ruins
themselves going wrong, and it must look expensive — if these effects feel
cheap, the game loses on sight. Prototype each one, judge it on screen, and cut
anything that does not slap.

The direction is decomposition, not desaturation — the Nothing, not a grey
filter. The ruins slowly come apart; the cells of the mass go fuzzy; the effect
exaggerates with depth. Any pallor stays subtle and the brightness hierarchy is
untouchable: enemies and the player must pop in every room.

### 8.3 The ladder

Written against the dials that exist. LEVELS §3.2b defines them and is the
authoring reference; this is what each room is *for*.

| room    | authored                              | reads as                          |
|---------|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Heap    | nothing                               | normal — the only room that is    |
| Sink    | `dissolve 0.35`, `oil`, `fray`        | a working floor that sweats       |
| Archive | `dissolve 0.22`, near-still churn     | held breath                       |
| Store   | `dissolve 1.0`, brisk churn           | no sharp edges left               |
| Cell    | `dissolve 1.6`, fastest churn         | boundaries meaningless            |

1. **The Heap — clean.** Solid blocks, gentle churn, clear grid, no air. This is
   what normal looks like, and it is the only room that gets to be normal. It
   authors nothing, which is why the global `SHELL` baseline must stay at zero:
   the Heap *is* the baseline, and a baseline parked high leaves the descent
   nowhere to go.
2. **The Sink — the sheen.** Thin-film interference on the *floor*, coming up
   through the seams, and the first loss of certainty at the edges. Denser grid,
   working tempo.
3. **The Archive — held breath.** Near-still churn at half tempo and low
   amplitude. Its decomposition sits *below* the Sink's on purpose: stillness is
   this room's texture, and wear that moves would spend it.
4. **The Store — advancing.** No sharp edge anywhere. This is where the roaming
   effects earn their place if they are ever authored per-room.
5. **The Cell — the Nothing.** Boundaries meaningless. Near-black; the only
   colour is the light through the final gate.

**And the same rooms get worse across the campaign.** `RunConfig.siteDecay` is a
floor the story raises per rung — zero for the first two shifts, 0.45 by the
last — added on top of whatever each room authored. The room-to-room gradient is
what the player reads inside one run; this is the one they only notice on the
third visit to a corridor they thought they knew. It is shallow and late by
design: climb it fast and every room ends up the same shade of ruined, which
costs the ladder above its whole job.

Held in reserve on purpose: the backdrop slabs behind everything keep drifting in
all five rooms. They stop for the first time in the ending (§11 of NARRATIVE),
and no room may spend that early.

### 8.4 Where it is built

The structure shader (`gfx/structure.ts`) owns both halves — the mass and the
floor it stands on — and therefore owns the descent. Per-room churn tempo and
block sizes are already data.

**Nothing is ever painted onto a block face.** Decided on screen, after trying
it: the blocks are flat value with hard borders and they must stay that way, so a
film laid over them reads as a stain on a shape no matter how the noise is tuned.
Two consequences, and they are the rule for every effect:

- **The mass's share of the descent is its outline.** `dissolve` trades a block's
  boundary for wisps — edges billow *past* themselves and the silhouette bleeds
  near-black tendrils into the room, so dissolving means getting bigger: matter
  leaving, not a cloud evaporating. `fray` is the finer, beat-synced version of
  the same idea, crumbling silhouettes in square nibbles that belong to the
  blocks' own grammar. Both are safe against "dilate, never erode" because the
  backing pass covers the collider whatever the drawn layers do.
- **Colour, shimmer and light go on the floor**, and come *up* through the seams.
  `oil` is animated thin-film interference on the ground: a drifting thickness
  field, a travelling swell deciding where it is thick, weighted cold — green is
  what a blue-black floor lifts most readily, and at equal weight the room goes
  swamp.

**A material belongs to a place, not to the camera.** `oil`, `fray` and
`dissolve` are resolved *per pixel* from world-space room rectangles, not
switched when the player crosses a threshold. Switching them globally repainted
the room the player had just left as they stepped through the gate, which is both
wrong and the most visible thing in the frame. `decay` and `shred` stay global
because they genuinely are events roaming the whole arena rather than properties
of one room.

`shred` is the one dial with a real frame cost — it widens the mass shader's
working region — so it is a late accent and never a baseline. The quality
governor (`gfx/quality.ts`) will trade bloom detail to pay for it.

**Not built.** Weather — the one permitted screen-space category, replacing the
forbidden field programs rather than joining them. Grid slip and contortion. The
Cell's coupling to the player's own output, so the room breathes with the engine.
A swarm that clusters silhouettes off the cap-distance field: `shred`'s flecks
*leave* the mass, which is close to §8.3's "flies" but not the same picture, and
the difference should be decided rather than blurred.
